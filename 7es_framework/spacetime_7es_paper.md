# Spacetime as Information Processor: A Systems-Theoretic Resolution of the Quantum Gravity Interface Problem

**Authors:** Clinton Alden, Principal Theorist
*Pre-print*

**Abstract**

We present a novel systems-theoretic analysis of spacetime using the 7ES (Element Structure) Framework, demonstrating that spacetime functions as an active information processing system rather than a passive geometric background. Through rigorous application of Fundamental Design Principles (FDPs) derived from natural systems optimization, we show that quantum mechanics and general relativity represent scale-dependent manifestations of unified spacetime information processing architecture. Our analysis yields measurable predictions for LIGO holographic noise signatures, JWST dark energy phase transitions, and quantum gravity correlations, while providing a mathematical framework for controlled spacetime engineering applications including Einstein-Rosen bridge formation.

**Keywords:** spacetime, information processing, quantum gravity, systems theory, holographic principle

## 1. Introduction

The persistent challenge of unifying quantum mechanics and general relativity has dominated theoretical physics for nearly a century (Weinberg, 1989; Rovelli, 2004). Despite remarkable successes in their respective domains, these frameworks remain fundamentally incompatible at scales approaching the Planck length (Wheeler, 1989). Recent developments in holographic duality (Maldacena, 1997; Witten, 1998) and emergent gravity theories (Verlinde, 2011) suggest that spacetime itself may possess computational properties, yet no systematic framework exists for analyzing spacetime as an active information processing system.

The 7ES (Element Structure) Framework provides a recursive, scale-invariant methodology for analyzing complex systems across 42+ orders of magnitude (Smith et al., 2024). Originally developed for institutional system analysis, the framework decomposes any system into seven fundamental elements: Input, Output, Processing, Controls, Feedback, Interface, and Environment. Each element functions as a subsystem governed by identical architectural principles, enabling fractal analysis from quantum to cosmological scales.

This paper applies the 7ES Framework to spacetime itself, demonstrating that apparent quantum-relativistic incompatibilities resolve when spacetime is understood as a unified information processing architecture rather than separate physical theories operating in the same geometric background.

8. **Intellectual Honesty:** System acknowledges and responds to operational limits

$$\text{FDP}(S) = \frac{1}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{8} \text{FDP}_i$$

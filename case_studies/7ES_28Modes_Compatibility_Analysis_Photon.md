### Element 1: INPUT (I) — How the Photon Comes Into Existence

The Input element of the photon describes the processes by which the photon is created and the conditions that specify its initial quantum state. This element exhibits pronounced multi-pathway structure.

Spontaneous emission occurs when an excited atom transitions to a lower energy state, releasing the energy difference as a photon. Stimulated emission occurs when an existing photon field induces this transition coherently. Bremsstrahlung generates photons from decelerating charged particles. Pair annihilation (e⁺e⁻ → γγ) converts rest mass energy entirely into photon energy. Synchrotron and Cherenkov radiation represent yet additional creation pathways with distinct physical origins. **I_I — Informational Input:** At creation, the photon is specified by a set of quantum numbers: frequency (ν), wavevector (k̂, specifying direction), polarization state (two helicity states, λ = ±1), and quantum phase (φ). **I_R — Relational Input:** The photon's creation is constrained by the relational structure of the emitting system. Conservation of angular momentum imposes selection rules (ΔJ = 0, ±1; Δm = 0, ±1) on atomic transitions. Parity conservation constrains which transitions can produce electric dipole radiation versus magnetic dipole or higher multipoles. Gauge invariance (the U(1) symmetry of QED) provides the deepest relational constraint: the photon exists *because* of this symmetry. These relational inputs are not a single constraint but a hierarchy of constraints at different levels of physical description.

**I_T — Temporal Input:** The timing of photon creation carries physical significance. In spontaneous emission, the emission time is stochastic, governed by the transition rate Γ = 1/τ, where τ is the excited-state lifetime. In stimulated emission, the timing is determined by the stimulating field. The coherence time of the source determines the temporal bandwidth of the photon wavepacket. For ultrashort laser pulses, the temporal input structure is directly engineered. The temporal input thus operates through at least two distinct mechanisms: stochastic (spontaneous) and deterministic (stimulated).

**Subsystem Assessment for Input:** Input exhibits at least six distinct energetic creation pathways, multiple independent informational channels, a hierarchy of relational constraints, and dual temporal mechanisms (stochastic vs. deterministic). ### Element 2: OUTPUT (O) — How the Photon Delivers Its Effects

**O_E — Energetic Output:** The photon delivers its energy to absorbing systems through mechanisms that are sharply energy-dependent. At low energies (up to ~100 keV for typical materials), the photoelectric effect dominates: the photon is fully absorbed and its entire energy transferred to an ejected electron. At intermediate energies (~0.1–10 MeV), Compton scattering dominates: the photon transfers partial energy to an electron and scatters with reduced frequency. At high energies (>1.022 MeV near a nucleus), pair production occurs: the photon is annihilated and its energy converted into an electron-positron pair. These three mechanisms represent genuinely distinct output subsystems, each with different physics, different cross-sections, and different environmental requirements (pair production requires a nearby nucleus for momentum conservation). The transition between regimes is not gradual blending but competitive dominance shifts.

**O_I — Informational Output:** The photon delivers its encoded information at detection. Measured quantities include frequency (via spectrometry), polarization (via polarimetry), direction (via detector geometry), and arrival time (via fast detectors). In quantum information contexts, single-photon states can carry qubits encoded in polarization, time-bin, orbital angular momentum, or path. Each encoding scheme constitutes a distinct informational output channel. The which-path information in interferometric setups represents yet another informational output mode, famously complementary to interference visibility.

**O_R — Relational Output:** The photon modifies the relational structure of the absorbing system. In the photoelectric effect, it breaks the electron-atom bond, altering the atomic configuration. In Compton scattering, it transfers momentum, changing the kinematic relationship between electron and nucleus. In pair production, it creates a new particle-antiparticle relationship where none existed. These are qualitatively different structural modifications. Additionally, radiation pressure and the photon's momentum transfer (p = ℏk) can alter spatial relationships of macroscopic objects (optical trapping, solar sails).

**O_T — Temporal Output:** The photon's arrival time completes a temporal measurement. The time-of-flight encodes distance information (radar, lidar). In quantum optics, photon arrival statistics (bunching for thermal light, antibunching for single-photon sources) carry temporal correlation information. The photon's finite coherence length creates a temporal output window. In laser cavities, the periodic output constitutes a timing signal (optical clocks achieve the highest precision of any human-made timing device).

**Subsystem Assessment for Output:** Output exhibits three energy-regime-dependent energetic pathways (photoelectric, Compton, pair production) that constitute fully distinct physical subsystems with different scaling laws and conservation requirements. Informational output operates through multiple independent encoding channels. This element is a branching multi-pathway system.
